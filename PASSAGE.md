# The reading passage

**Read this out loud, normally, at your own pace. About 6 minutes.**

Read it the way you'd read a story to your child — warm, unhurried, with your normal
expression. Let the questions sound like questions and the exclamations sound like
exclamations. That variation is the whole point; a flat read teaches the computer a
flat voice.

Pause a moment between paragraphs. If you stumble, just pause and start that sentence
again. Take a break at any of the section breaks.

This passage is built to contain every sound in English, several times over, so that
nothing is missing later. That's why some of the sentences are a little odd.

---

## One — Morning

Good morning. It's a bright, breezy Thursday, and the whole house is awake far too
early. Sunshine comes through the yellow curtains and lands in a warm square on the
floor. Somewhere downstairs the kettle begins to sing, and the old dog thumps his tail
against the kitchen door.

Are you hungry? Would you like eggs, or just toast and jam? There's fresh orange juice
in the jug, and enough honey left for one more spoonful. Careful — that plate is hot.

I put your blue shoes by the stairs, next to the yellow umbrella. Where did you leave
your other sock? Under the bed again, I should think.

## Two — The rescue

Out beyond the harbour, past the tall gray lighthouse, a small boat had lost its
engine. The coastguard radio crackled: three people aboard, no injuries, drifting south.

Within the hour a rescue crew was launched. They wore thick orange jackets and heavy
boots, and they moved with the calm of people who had done this a thousand times. The
sea was rough, and the wind was pushing hard against them, but they knew exactly what
to do.

"Hold on!" the captain shouted over the noise. "We're nearly there!"

And they were. Every single person came home safe that evening, and the whole village
cheered them in from the quay. It was a genuine pleasure to watch. What a day that was!

## Three — By the sea

The beach in early June is my favorite place on earth. There's treasure everywhere if
you're willing to look: smooth green glass, a curl of rope, a feather, a purple shell
worn thin by the water.

We measure the day in small pleasures. Sandy toes. A shared bag of chips. The particular
joy of a cold drink when you're thirsty. The pleasure of doing absolutely nothing at all.

The boys are making a tremendous noise down by the point, and their voices carry clear
across the water. Somebody's lost a coin in the sand and the whole gang has joined in
to look for it. What a racket. What a choice of way to spend an afternoon.

Do you remember last year, when that enormous wave caught us both and we couldn't stop
laughing? Your father swore he'd never go near the sea again. He went back the following
Tuesday.

## Four — In the kitchen

Baking is mostly patience. You measure the flour, you soften the butter, you beat the
eggs until they're pale and thick. Then you wait.

Three hundred grams of sugar. Half a teaspoon of salt. A pinch of cinnamon, if you like
it — I usually do. Push it gently into the corners of the tin, smooth the top, and slide
it onto the middle shelf.

Twenty-five minutes, maybe thirty. The kitchen fills with that unmistakable smell, sweet
and warm and a little bit burnt at the edges, and everyone finds a reason to wander
through and ask whether it's ready yet.

It isn't. Not quite. But nearly.

## Five — Questions

Why is the sky blue? Where do birds sleep? How long is a year, really? Who decided
that Wednesday should be spelled that way?

Which would you rather: fly like a bird, or breathe underwater like a fish?

Are we there yet? Is it time? Can I stay up? Just five more minutes — please?

## Six — Evening

The light goes soft and orange, and the house grows quiet. Your brother is upstairs
singing something tuneless in the bath. The dog has given up and gone to sleep across
the doorway, exactly where everybody needs to walk.

Bring me the book with the yellow cover — the one about the boy and the enormous
elephant. We'll read three chapters, and then I mean it, that's genuinely enough for
one night.

Can you hear that? The rain has appeared out of nowhere, blown in near the pier, and
it's clear we'll be indoors for the rest of the year. Come here to me.

Usually we'd put the television on, but this evening we made a different decision, and
it's been a real pleasure. There's treasure in an ordinary evening, if you measure it
right.

Are your teeth brushed? Show me. Good.

Sleep well, my love. Tomorrow we'll do all of it again.

---

*That's everything. Thank you — this is the part that makes all the rest of it sound
like you.*
